Your report is already strong on market behavior and strategic insights. To expand it into a full competitor analysis—especially for comparing aspects like SEO, website, pricing, funding, and key personnel—I’d recommend structuring your spreadsheet into several clear sections. Here’s a step‐by‐step approach:

### 1. Establish Key Data Points and Categories

**A. Company Overview**  
• **Basic Info:** Include founding year, CEO, headquarters/location, and total employee count (sources: LinkedIn, Crunchbase, Glassdoor).  
• **Funding Details:** Note funding rounds, investors, total funding amount, and, if possible, estimated revenue (using a formula such as “Headcount × $150,000–$200,000” as a starting point).

**B. Product & Service Offerings**  
• **Core Features & Differentiators:** List product features, unique selling propositions (e.g., voice-driven admin, mobile-first interface).  
• **Pricing Strategy:** Record subscription tiers, pricing range (monthly/annual), free trial availability, and any add-on services.

**C. Digital Presence & SEO**  
• **Website Analysis:** Evaluate website design, navigation, mobile-friendliness, page speed, and UX. Tools like BuiltWith can help you determine technical stacks.  
• **SEO Performance:** Include keywords they rank for, domain authority, backlink profiles, and overall SEO strategy insights (using SEMrush, Ahrefs, Moz).  
• **Content Strategy:** Note frequency and type of content (blogs, case studies, etc.) and engagement levels (social shares, user reviews).

**D. Go-to-Market and Strategic Positioning**  
• **Marketing Channels:** How do they approach digital marketing (paid ads, social media, partnerships)?  
• **Market Approach:** Identify their customer segments, distribution channels, and any local/industry associations leveraged.

**E. Strengths & Weaknesses (SWOT Snapshot)**  
• A brief qualitative summary that highlights each competitor’s main strengths and potential gaps relative to your value proposition.

### 2. Build a Structured Spreadsheet

Create separate tabs or sections for each cluster of competitors if needed. For each competitor, include columns such as:  
• Name  
• Company Overview (founding year, CEO, location, employee count)  
• Funding & Revenue Estimate  
• Product Features & Pricing  
• Website/SEO Metrics (domain authority, top keywords, backlink count)  
• Digital Presence (social media engagement, content frequency)  
• Market Approach & Partnerships  
• SWOT Highlights  
• Notes/Observations (any unique elements like upcoming hires or strategic shifts)

### 3. Gather Data from Multiple Sources

Use a mix of publicly available tools and sources:  
• **Crunchbase/LinkedIn/Glassdoor** for company background, funding, and employee data.  
• **SEMrush, Ahrefs, Moz** for SEO and website metrics.  
• **BuiltWith/WhatRuns** for website technology.  
• **Competitor Websites** and **Ad Libraries** for pricing, content, and marketing tactics.

### 4. Keep It Actionable and Iterative

• **Prioritize Deep Dives:** Focus on the top 5–7 competitors who represent the greatest competitive threat, and keep a lighter touch on the rest.  
• **Regular Updates:** Competitor landscapes evolve—plan to update the spreadsheet periodically to maintain accuracy.  
• **Visual Summaries:** Consider adding charts or a strategy canvas (like the Blue Ocean Strategy grid) to quickly visualize gaps and opportunities.

### 5. Use the Analysis for Strategic Recommendations

Once you’ve compiled the data:  
• Identify common weaknesses or gaps that Constructiv AI can exploit (e.g., competitors lacking a robust mobile-first or voice interaction feature).  
• Benchmark pricing and digital presence to validate your product’s positioning.  
• Translate insights into clear next steps—whether that’s tweaking your SEO strategy, refining the onboarding process, or targeting a specific customer segment more aggressively.

By structuring your analysis this way, you not only have a data-rich report but also a living document that guides strategic decision-making. It becomes a comprehensive tool for both understanding the current market and identifying actionable opportunities for growth.
